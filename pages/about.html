About Me

Hi, I'm Karen! I study BEngSc Digital Arts at Wits, which is the kind of degree that takes a full sentence to explain but makes complete sense once you do. It's a collaboration between the School of Electrical and Information Engineering and the Wits School of Arts — so one week I'm doing circuits and signal processing, and the next I'm designing game mechanics and thinking about what makes an interaction feel good. Three years in, those two halves have stopped feeling like separate things. Which I think is the whole point.
I care a lot about how things feel to use. Not just how they look, not just whether they work but whether the person using them has to think hard, get confused or gives up halfway. That's the problem I'm always trying to solve, whether I'm designing a game mechanic, building a webpage or training microprocessors in the electronics lab.
When I'm not studying or building something, I'm probably gaming, crocheting or drawing characters that'll never make it into an actual game. All three have made me better at my work in ways I didn't expect.

Where I come from

My parents are Taiwanese. I was born in Joburg, grew up in Joburg, and I'll probably always be a Joburg person at heart — but Taiwan is in me too, in ways that are harder to explain.
I went to The King's College in Bryanston for primary school, then Fourways High for secondary. Both in the northern suburbs, both formative in completely different ways. High school is where I first started actually tinkering with technology rather than just consuming it — and once I started, I couldn't stop.
That's eventually what pointed me toward Wits and toward BEngSc Digital Arts — a degree that sits right at the crossing point of engineering and art and doesn't let you be comfortable with just one. It made sense. Growing up across two cultures taught me to read situations quickly, adapt without making a big deal of it and never assume my perspective is the default one. I think that bleeds into how I design, even when I'm not aware of it.
