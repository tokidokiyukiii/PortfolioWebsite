Experiences

My work history covers warehouses, restaurants, and a nougat market stall — not the most obvious background for someone going into tech. But in every single one of those jobs, I found something that could work better and I tried to make it so. That habit is probably the most honest thing I can tell you about how I work.

Gemoto

Assistant Developer

I joined Gemoto as part of their operations team, but my role ended up being a lot more interesting than the job description suggested. I built a label printing system from scratch to cut down on the repetitive manual work in packaging, put together a task log so the team could actually track what was getting done, and created an invoice list with a simple built-in chat system so people could follow up without switching apps.
Working in a real warehouse environment taught me something I've held onto since: software has to earn its keep. Clever doesn't matter if it breaks under pressure. Reliable does.

Asian Herbivore

Waiter & Operations Support

At Asian Herbivore I was officially a waiter, but I spent a chunk of my time there helping get their digital ordering and delivery system off the ground. I helped set up and test the ordering workflow, making sure it actually fit the way the restaurant operated rather than forcing the restaurant to change for the software.
Running both things at the same time — customers in front of you, a system to sort out in the background — turns out to be decent training for switching contexts quickly without losing focus on either.

De Chen

Sales and System Support

De Chen is a small Taiwanese nougat business, and for me it was also a family one — so I cared about doing it well for more than one reason. Alongside the actual selling, I built a stock tracking system and a profit calculator in spreadsheet form so the business could see its numbers properly without doing everything by hand on paper.
It didn't have to be a perfect piece of software. It needed to be something people using it would actually trust and understand. That constraint taught me more about design than a lot of formal coursework did.
